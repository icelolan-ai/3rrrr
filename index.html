Ghost Cube

The Mechanics

The Physics Mechanics of the Ghost Cube

A modified cubic puzzle utilizing an internal 3x3x3 core with slanted and offset cutting planes, the Ghost Cube is more complex than a standard Rubik's cube due to its geometric asymmetry, mass distribution, and uneven pressure. The relevant physics principles and formulas are as follows:

1. Rotational Dynamics & Center of Mass Offset

Although the internal structure uses a 3x3x3 core, the asymmetrical piece cuts cause the Center of Mass (CM) of individual pieces and layers to shift out of alignment with the axis of rotation.

Position of the center of mass for a rotating layer ($R_{cm}$):

$$R_{cm} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i r_i$$

Where $m_i$ is the mass of each sub-piece, and $r_i$ is the position vector from the rotation axis. Because $R_{cm}$ does not lie on the axis of rotation, an unbalanced torque is generated, requiring the player to apply varying amounts of torque ($\tau$) at different angles of rotation.

Complex Moment of Inertia ($I$):

Due to the variety and overlap of piece shapes, the total moment of inertia changes dynamically with the rotation angle ($\theta$), resulting in uneven rotational resistance.

2. Contact Pressure & Tribology

The contact surfaces of a Ghost Cube are not flat planes like a standard Rubik's cube; instead, they feature stepped and slanted cuts, leading to an uneven pressure distribution across contact areas.

Contact Pressure ($P$):

$$P = \frac{F_N}{A_{eff}}$$

Where $F_N$ is the normal force and $A_{eff}$ is the effective contact area. Because certain pieces have very narrow contact areas ($A_{eff}$), pressure ($P$) spikes significantly in specific spots.

Local Frictional Force ($F_f$):

$$F_f = \mu P A_{eff}$$

High pressure in narrow regions causes high local static friction, leading to a higher tendency for "binding" or lock-up compared to standard cubes if forced off-axis.

3. Elasticity & Mechanical Interference

A defining characteristic of the Ghost Cube is that in its "solved" state, the pieces remain offset and misaligned. This makes the mechanism heavily reliant on the core spring's elasticity.

Hooke's Law & Potential Energy Storage ($U$): when twisting offset layers, wedge-shaped pieces push against each other perpendicularly, causing the core to expand and forcing the springs to stretch by a distance $x$.

$$F_{spring} = -kx, \quad U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$$

Where $k$ is the spring constant. This elastic potential energy acts as a dampening snap-back force to guide pieces back into alignment as they approach correct positions.

Mechanical Interference Condition:

Piece movement occurs only when the applied torque overcomes collision forces between material surfaces and spring tension. The bevel angle ($\theta_b$) of the pieces must correspond to the depth of the cut to prevent permanent mechanical lock-ups during rotation.

Rubik's Cube
(3x3x3)

The Mechanics

The Physics Mechanics of the 3x3x3 Rubik's Cube

Involves several branches of physics, including rotational dynamics, friction and tribology, lubricant fluid dynamics, and material elasticity. The relevant details and formulas are as follows:

1. Rotational Dynamics

Twisting a layer of the cube involves rotating an object in 3D space, governed by torque, moment of inertia, and angular acceleration.

Torque ($\tau$): the rotational force applied by the player to turn a layer around an axis.

$$\tau = r \times F = I\alpha$$

Where $r$ is the distance from the pivot point (core), $F$ is the applied force, $I$ is the moment of inertia, and $\alpha$ is the angular acceleration.

Moment of Inertia ($I$): since a Rubik's cube consists of multiple sub-pieces (edges, corners, centers) with distributed mass, the total moment of inertia is the sum of the mass of each piece multiplied by the square of its distance from the axis of rotation.

$$I = \sum m_i r_i^2$$

2. Friction & Tribology

Smooth turning performance depends on friction between ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene) plastic surfaces and lubricants.

Frictional Force ($F_f$): the resistance encountered during movement between contact surfaces.

$$F_f = \mu N$$

Where $\mu$ is the coefficient of friction (reduced by lubricants) and $N$ is the normal reaction force between surfaces. Lubricants lower $\mu$ and bridge the gap between static friction ($\mu_s$) and kinetic friction ($\mu_k$), ensuring smooth turning and preventing stick-slip phenomena.

3. Elasticity & Spring Tension

Modern Rubik's cube cores feature screws and springs on all six central axes to control tension, flexibility, and stability.

Hooke's Law: when turning the cube or when pieces push outward, the core springs stretch or compress proportionally to the applied force.

$$F = -kx$$

Where $F$ is the restoring force of the spring, $k$ is the spring constant, and $x$ is the displacement from equilibrium.

Elastic Potential Energy ($U$): energy stored in the springs as pieces are pushed outward.

$$U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$$

4. Corner Cutting Dynamics

The ability to turn a layer even when the faces are not 100% aligned is driven by repulsive forces between the bevel edges of corner and edge pieces.

When pieces collide, the diagonal force vectors resolve into perpendicular components that overcome spring tension ($F = -kx$), causing the core to expand slightly. This allows pieces to bypass alignment barriers via temporary elastic deformation of the plastic and springs.
